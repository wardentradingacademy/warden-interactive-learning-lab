DECISION 01 / 05

Two traders make $500 on the same ES move. Which conclusion is justified?

Trader A entered without a plan and moved a stop farther away. Trader B entered with defined evidence, a protective stop, and appropriate position size.
Trader B followed a defined process. The same favorable outcome does not make both decisions equally sound. Moving a stop farther away without a risk plan can turn a small loss into an uncontrolled one.
DECISION 02 / 05

ES breaks above a two-hour range. The five-minute breakout candle has not closed. What is the best-supported action given no other evidence?

The initial breakout alone does not establish whether buyers can hold control. Waiting for more information is an active, defensible decision. Neither continuation nor failure is guaranteed.
DECISION 03 / 05

Which statement best describes invalidation?

Invalidation is tied to the thesis and evidence, not wishful thinking or discomfort. Risk controls should be planned before entering, not improvised to avoid accepting a loss.
DECISION 04 / 05

A trader follows a justified plan, risks $200, and exits at the predefined stop. What can we conclude?

A planned loss can reflect sound execution. One losing trade does not determine whether a strategy has a long-term advantage; repeated evidence is needed.
DECISION 05 / 05

Which preparation most closely reflects a grandmaster's approach?

ES is rotating between fictional support at 5990 and resistance at 6010.
Preparing multiple scenarios helps a trader respond to evidence instead of defending a prediction. Location, expectation, confirmation, invalidation, and patience are central to the Warden process.
