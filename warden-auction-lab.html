Where does this example sit?

The highlighted condition compares total volume with absolute open-to-close displacement. It does not use delta sign or directional efficiency to choose a category.

High effort · Large directional progress

Investigate initiative and continuation. Does progress sustain through a meaningful boundary?

High effort · Little net progress

Investigate opposing liquidity, balance, or absorption. Check price-level executions and the subsequent response.

Low effort · Large directional progress

Investigate thin liquidity or limited opposition. Modest volume can accompany a substantial move.

Low effort · Little net progress

Investigate inactivity, contraction, or balance. Compare with the session and time-of-day context.

Training benchmarks only: “high effort” means at least 5,000 contracts in this five-minute example; “large progress” means at least 4.00 points of absolute open-to-close displacement. These chosen cutoffs organize the exercise. They are not market standards or trading thresholds. A live comparison needs a relevant baseline.

Three comparisons to practice

Use the presets and controls above, then record what you observed. These are study prompts, not automatically graded decisions.

Same candle, different effort

Compare “High effort · Large move” with “Low effort · Large move.” What changes? What stays identical?

Review the comparison

The candle is identical: +10 points, a 12-point range, and 83.3% efficiency. Volume changes from 10,000 to 2,000. The efficiency formula contains no volume, so it cannot tell you how much participation produced the move.

Same effort, different shape

Load “High effort · Large move.” Keep volume, open, and close fixed. Raise the high from 5,257 to 5,266. How does the efficiency reading change?

Review the comparison

Volume stays at 10,000 and displacement stays at +10 points. Range increases from 12 to 21 points, so efficiency falls from 83.3% to 47.6%. The effort–result category stays the same because this map uses volume and body displacement, not efficiency.

Same result, different aggression

Load “Buying delta · Falling price.” Change the buying share from 70% to 30% without changing price or total volume. What can you establish?

Review the comparison

Delta changes from +4,000 to −4,000. Price still falls 5 points and efficiency remains 71.4%. The first setting shows buying aggression without upward progress. Neither setting reveals participant identity, the intrabar sequence, or what price does next.
