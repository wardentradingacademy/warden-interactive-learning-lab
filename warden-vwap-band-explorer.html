1. Read the three inputs

VWAP is the center reference. A dispersion measure describes the spread of the included trading prices. A multiplier places a band one, two, or three dispersion units from the center.

In this model: VWAP, the dispersion input σ, and current price are assumed values you control. They are not estimates calculated from a live market or transaction history. The model calculates band positions from those inputs. It does not generate trade signals or reversal probabilities.

Upper band = VWAP + multiplier × σ.
Lower band = VWAP − multiplier × σ.

2. Move the inputs and watch the bands

Start with the narrow and wider presets. Current price and VWAP stay the same, while the assumed dispersion changes. Then use the sliders. Keyboard users can focus a slider and press the arrow keys.

3. Guided experiments

Make a prediction, perform the action above, then reveal the explanation.

Experiment A — Same price, narrow versus wider bands

Select “Narrow bands.” With VWAP 5,250, σ 5, and price 5,270, price is above the upper 3σ band at 5,265. Now select “Wider bands.” Price is still 5,270 and VWAP is still 5,250, but the 1σ upper band is 5,265 and the 2σ upper band is 5,280. Price is now between them. The market price did not move; your assumed dispersion changed.

Experiment B — Place price exactly on a band

Select “At upper 2σ.” Price is 5,270: 20 points above VWAP, with σ 10. That identifies its position at the calculated upper 2σ band. It does not establish a short entry or a required return to VWAP.

Experiment C — Compare the lower side

Select “At lower 2σ.” Price is 5,230, at the lower 2σ band. The construction is symmetric around VWAP. The auction’s future behavior is not guaranteed to be symmetric: a lower band touch does not guarantee a rally.

Experiment D — Change the center while price stays fixed

Select “At upper 2σ,” then move the assumed VWAP slider to 5,260. Current price remains 5,270 and σ remains 10. Price is now at upper 1σ. The center changed; price did not. In a live chart, both the average and the dispersion can develop as new trading enters the calculation.

4. The regime still matters

Directional auction

Price advances, pullbacks retain ground, and further progress develops. Trading near or beyond an upper band can accompany this strength. Distance alone does not establish reversal.

Rotational auction

Opposing swings revisit the same area. Price may travel between bands, but the next band touch is not guaranteed to reverse. Observe the response.

Changing auction

A failed extension or loss of relevant structure adds information beyond band location. Evaluate what changed and where that interpretation would become invalid.

Explain before returning to Tutor: Why can the same current price be beyond 3σ under one dispersion input but inside 2σ under another? Why does neither position give you a guaranteed next move?
