Resistance is a question, not an answer

You enter a fictional ES market approaching a repeatedly tested area. Mark the structural zone and describe bullish and bearish possibilities before the breakout appears. Then decide one completed five-minute candle at a time.

You will work through continuation, failed-breakout, and rotational-balance teaching cases in a shuffled order. They begin with identical information. These are matched teaching cases, not independent unseen exam items; earlier outcomes may influence later expectations.

The written exercise plan

  • At the approach and initial breakout: this plan requires waiting or standing aside. It does not permit buying the first breakout candle or fading it immediately.
  • Long setup: a completed close above the working resistance zone, another completed candle holding above it, then a completed pullback that stays above the zone and closes above the preceding candle’s close.
  • Short setup: a completed breakout, a completed return below the working zone, then a completed retest into that zone that closes below the return candle’s close. This is a tactical failed-breakout trade, not a claim that the four-hour trend reversed.
  • Invalidation: a long stop must be below its completed retest low. A short stop must be above its completed retest high. Exact stops must be on 0.25-point ticks. A different strategy might use a different invalidation; this exercise checks this defined retest thesis.
  • Objectives: choose from known references and justify the path. Long targets cannot exceed 5220, the near edge of a previously identified higher-timeframe resistance area. Short targets cannot be below the known session low at 5184. The zone and 5200 reference can be intervening areas.
  • Risk: maximum estimated risk $250 per independent proposal. Whole MES contracts at $5 per point, plus a fictional $5 per-contract round-trip allowance for costs and slippage. Minimum planned net reward/risk is 1:1 after that allowance. Neither stops nor allowances guarantee the realized loss.
  • Any setup, stop, target, or risk breach prevents a passing mechanical process check, even if later price moves favorably. Waiting and standing aside remain valid at every checkpoint.
