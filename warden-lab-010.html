The four-hour structure is bullish. The sixty-minute chart has been correcting lower. On the five-minute chart, a possible recovery begins near a previously identified support area.

Work through two matched cases, each with three decision checkpoints. One recovery develops; the other fails. Their order is shuffled at the start. These are paired teaching cases, not independent unseen assessment items: knowing the first outcome can influence your expectations for the second.

Choose using current evidence. Your decision locks before the next candle appears. Waiting or standing aside at one checkpoint keeps later decisions available.

The written exercise plan

  • Strategy: bullish continuation only, within the supplied directional four-hour context. A long proposal requires a completed five-minute reclaim of the support area at 5088–5092, followed by a completed candle that stays above 5092 and closes above the reclaim candle’s close. A failed long thesis does not authorize a short under this plan.
  • Invalidation: this particular setup uses a stop below the rejection low at 5088. Stop prices must be valid 0.25-point ticks. A closer stop does not satisfy this exercise’s thesis.
  • Objective: choose a justified reference from the board. The four-hour open at 5100 is an intervening reference; the developing high already reached at 5120 is a possible farther objective. Neither is a guaranteed destination. Do not place a target beyond 5120 in this supplied case.
  • Risk: maximum estimated risk is $250 per proposal. Use whole MES contracts at $5 per point, including a fictional $5 per-contract round-trip allowance for costs and slippage. This estimate does not guarantee a loss limit.
  • Economics: minimum planned net reward/risk is 1:1 after that allowance. Any setup, stop, objective, or risk breach prevents a passing mechanical process check, regardless of later direction.
  • No scheduled announcement or exceptional execution restriction is supplied during this fictional window. In real trading, check those conditions independently.
