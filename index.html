Two Traders. One Market.

Chapter 1 · The Market Is a Game of Decisions

Your assignment: An ES futures market has been trading between 6000 and 6020. Price has just broken above 6020. What do you know, and what do you still need to know?

This diagram shows only information available at the decision point. It does not reveal what happens next.

Explore the two approaches

Trader A — React immediately

Trader A buys the moment price crosses 6020 because the breakout looks exciting. The trader has not defined what successful continuation should look like, where the idea becomes invalid, or how much to risk.

Discussion: Even if this trade wins, is the decision process repeatable and controlled?

Trader B — Evaluate the position

Trader B identifies 6020 as the former range high. The trader prepares two scenarios: buyers sustain control above 6020, or price falls back into the range and exposes late breakout buyers. Trader B waits for evidence and defines invalidation before considering risk.

Discussion: Waiting is an active decision, not a prediction that the breakout must fail.

Instructor's guide — Reveal after deciding

Strongest supported action at this moment: WAIT. We do not yet know whether buyers can sustain control above 6020. A planned breakout entry might still be valid under a different strategy, but no entry criteria or risk plan have been supplied here.

What to watch: Acceptance above the level, a failed retest, or a return inside the range. These are observations, not guaranteed outcomes.

Your decision journal

Before opening the instructor's guide, write down: (1) Buy, sell, or wait? (2) What would confirm your expectation? (3) What would invalidate it? (4) What risk would be acceptable?

This activity does not automatically collect or grade responses. Students can record answers in their own journal until Academy assessment integration is enabled.

THE GRANDMASTER PRINCIPLE

The goal is not to predict the next candle. It is to understand the position, prepare for competing outcomes, and accept risk only when the decision is justified.
