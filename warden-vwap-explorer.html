Price tells you where trading occurred.
Volume tells you how much weight it carries.

Learn the idea. Move the volume. Watch the average change.

The purpose of this lesson

In Chapter 12, you studied participation: how much trading takes place and what that effort produces. Now we connect participation to price. Two prices may appear equally important on a chart, yet one may represent ten times as many executed contracts as the other.

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to explain VWAP in plain language, show why unequal volume changes an average, and distinguish a useful reference price from a reason to enter a trade.

The question VWAP answers:
Since the selected starting point, what is the average traded price when each contract receives equal weight?

1. Start with the name

VWAP means Volume-Weighted Average Price. “Weighted” means that a price associated with more executed volume has more influence on the result.

Price

A transaction occurs at an agreed price. In this lesson, our hypothetical ES prices are 5,240, 5,250, and 5,260.

Volume

Volume counts contracts traded. One executed contract has both a buyer and a seller; it counts as one contract of volume.

Imagine one contract trading at 5,240 and nine contracts trading at 5,260. Averaging the two price labels gives 5,250. Averaging the prices of all ten contracts gives 5,258. The second answer gives each contract a voice.

Grandmaster principle: More volume gives a price more influence on VWAP. It does not automatically give that price more predictive power.

2. Understand it before calculating it

Think of VWAP as the balance point of executed trading prices. Add more weight at a lower price and the balance point moves lower. Add more weight at a higher price and it moves higher.

That balance point can sit between actual trading prices. An average does not need to be a price at which a transaction occurred.

The calculation, in words

Multiply each execution price by the number of contracts traded there. Add those products. Divide by the total number of contracts.

VWAP = Sum of (Price × Volume) ÷ Total Volume

At the transaction level, this uses execution prices. Bar-based indicators may instead use a representative price such as (High + Low + Close) ÷ 3, multiplied by each bar’s volume. That is an approximation to transaction-level VWAP; results depend on the data and settings.

4. Guided practice — make the relationship visible

Perform each experiment in the model above, then open its explanation.

Experiment 1 • Give each price the same volume

Select “Equal volume.” Each group has 100 contracts. VWAP is 5,250 because the three prices have equal influence. Here, the volume-weighted average and the equal-weight average match.

Experiment 2 • Move the weight to the higher price

Select “More at the high.” The volumes are 100, 100, and 800. Group C now represents 80% of total volume. VWAP becomes 5,257, although none of the price labels changed. It stays below 5,260 because 200 contracts still traded lower.

Experiment 3 • Move the weight to the lower price

Select “More at the low.” VWAP becomes 5,243. The final execution group still trades at 5,260, but the average remains closer to 5,240. The last price and the average price answer different questions.

Experiment 4 • Double every volume

Set the sliders to 100, 200, and 300. VWAP is approximately 5,253.33. Now set them to 200, 400, and 600. VWAP stays the same. Total volume doubled, but each group’s share of the total stayed identical. Relative weight determines the average.

Experiment 5 • Remove participation

Select “Zero volume.” There is no VWAP because there are no contracts to average. Now increase only Group B. VWAP becomes 5,250 and stays there as you add volume at that same price.

5. Why the starting point matters

VWAP accumulates from a defined starting point. A session VWAP resets according to the configured session. An anchored VWAP begins at a selected event or time and includes eligible trading after that anchor.

Two traders can display different VWAP values on the same market if their starting points, session templates, price sources, or data differ. Before comparing lines, compare what each calculation includes.

Ask first: “Average price since when?”
For ES, a VWAP covering an overnight session and one beginning at the U.S. cash open answer different questions.

As accumulated volume grows, a fixed-size new execution generally has less influence. A new trade above the existing VWAP pulls it upward; a new trade below it pulls it downward. The size of that pull depends on the new volume relative to the volume already accumulated.

6. Read the reference without inventing a signal

ObservationWhat it establishes
Current price above VWAPCurrent price is above the selected volume-weighted average.
Current price below VWAPCurrent price is below that average.
Price repeatedly crosses VWAPPrice is moving back and forth around the reference.

These observations need market structure, location, and subsequent behavior before they become part of a trading decision. Being above VWAP does not guarantee continuation. Being far below it does not require a return to the average.

VWAP does not identify institutional traders, reveal everyone’s entry price, or prove that most participants are profitable. It is neither the most frequently traded price nor a count of buyers versus sellers. It summarizes executed price and volume over the selected interval.

The Grandmaster’s question:
Where is price relative to the average, what trading does that average include, and how is the market responding there?
